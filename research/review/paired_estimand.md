# Formal estimand: paired predictive updates conditional on a future logged item

This note defines the quantity the fixed-probe experiment can estimate. It does not assert a novel theorem or identify a causal learning effect.

## Objects and population

Let D denote the fixed training data and fitted pipeline, including item parameters. For student u and an observed contiguous episode i, H0 is the full information available immediately after the first submission; H1 is the full information after the last submission of that same episode. The observed subsequent episode has item identity J and first observed finalized outcome Y_J in {0,1}. In the current diagnostic J is supplied to both queries retrospectively.

Let S=1 require all of the following: i has at least two submissions; a subsequent episode is observed; J differs from i; J has not previously been observed for this student; J is supported by the prescribed training item bank; the target label is usable. Dataset-specific definitions must say whether TEST exposure and missing-label submissions count as prior exposure. Let C specify the extract and cohort selection, including eligibility based on full-history activity.

Measurement qualification: in ACcoding, event order is based on submission `created_at` and an ID tiebreak; verdict-release times are not verified. Operationally the implementation constructs prefixes of earlier recorded submissions with their archived verdicts. Calling these literally available online histories requires an additional assumption that the corresponding verdicts had already been released at each checkpoint. Prefix-invariance tests cannot establish that timing assumption. The retrospective predictive estimand remains well-defined; real-time deployment availability is a separate measurement claim.

For a fixed predictor f_D, define p_k=f_D(H_k,J), k in {0,1}, and loss difference

    d_ui = ell(p_1,Y_J) - ell(p_0,Y_J).

Negative d is an improvement in prediction error for this same observed outcome. The natural pair-weighted estimand of the current pooled statistic is

    Delta_D,C = E_C[sum_{i:S_ui=1} d_ui | D] / E_C[M_u | D],

where M_u is the number of retained pairs for student u. Its estimator is sum d_ui / sum M_u. Equivalently, it describes the selected law of a retained pair, which weights students proportionally to retained-pair counts. It is not the same as E_C[(1/M_u) sum d_ui | M_u>0,D], the equal-student mean.

Student-cluster bootstrap resamples students and retains all their pairs. This handles within-student dependence under the stated sampling view; it does not remove selection, change the estimand to equal-student weighting, account for retraining, or establish platform/curriculum generalization. A deterministic hash cohort is a reproducible sampling convention, not a randomized educational intervention.

## What proper-score improvement means under an ideal model

Work under P*, the selected-pair distribution conditional on D,C,S=1. For the full histories define nested sigma-fields

    G0 = sigma(H0,J),   G1 = sigma(H1,J),   G0 subset G1,
    r_k = E*[Y_J | G_k].

The earlier forecast concerns the same eventual Y_J as the later forecast. Therefore the tower property gives

    E*[r_1-r_0 | G0] = 0.

This identity holds even if genuine learning occurs between H0 and H1. The earlier ideal forecast already averages over future learning and events that precede the same eventual assessment. It does NOT compare current mastery before and after a learning transition.

For Bayes-optimal Brier forecasts,

    R_Brier(r_1) - R_Brier(r_0) = - E*[(r_1-r_0)^2].

For Bayes-optimal log-loss forecasts,

    R_log(r_1) - R_log(r_0) = - I*(Y_J; H1 | H0,J).

Thus proper-score improvement is the value of extra predictive information under the selected law. It can arise without any change in latent competence. Conversely, zero mean forecast update is compatible with genuine learning. Neither result alone determines an instructional action's effect.

If positive parts of forecast updates are treated as rewards, then for this ideal fixed-future-target setting

    E*[(r_1-r_0)_+] = (1/2) E*[|r_1-r_0|].

Positive reward can therefore be generated by information alone. This is a diagnostic against calling that quantity “learning gain”; information can still have useful downstream decision value.

Do not generalize this result to every evolving latent-mastery model. If K_t and K_{t+1} are genuinely different latent states in a correctly specified transition model, and m_t=E[K_t|F_t], then

    E[m_{t+1}-m_t | F_t] = E[K_{t+1}-K_t | F_t].

That is a model-based expected transition gain under its assumptions, not a posterior martingale for one fixed latent variable or one fixed future outcome. The present real-log probe does not identify that transition or validate the assumptions. A critique must name the exact reward, state semantics and model, rather than asserting that all posterior-change rewards are mathematically invalid.

## Actual model errors and compressed states

The fitted logistic/KT forecasts are not guaranteed to equal these conditional expectations. For Brier score,

    Delta R = -E*[(r_1-r_0)^2]
              + E*[(p_1-r_1)^2 - (p_0-r_0)^2].

For log loss, replace the squared approximation errors by KL(Bernoulli(r_k) || Bernoulli(p_k)). Observed changes mix additional information with changes in misspecification/calibration. There is no guarantee that the actual p_k obeys the martingale identity or improves with additional events.

The rolling summary vectors in the pilot are also not nested information sets. H1 as a full history contains H0, but a last-five-response mean at H1 can discard information present in its H0 counterpart. The ideal information identities apply to full nested histories (or explicitly nested feature sets), not automatically to two compressed vectors. They provide an interpretation reference, not a performance guarantee for the implemented estimator.

## A task-alignment problem in the exploratory implementation

An ordinary first-attempt model is trained to predict an outcome at its immediate pre-submission history. The H1 query matches that timing. H0 queries the same model earlier, with multiple intervening submissions before Y_J. Restricting training targets to first attempts corrects outcome-type mismatch but does not fully correct horizon, stopping, or selected-population mismatch.

The original frozen-predictor comparison remains a valid audit of how that specific readout behaves when reused as a progress proxy. It is not an estimate of Bayes incremental information, and changes should not be attributed solely to a learner-state mechanism.

A prespecified control constructs the same eligible H0/H1 pairs among training students and fits a pipeline for this exact future Y_J. A pooled design can use rows (features(H0,J),phase=0,Y_J) and (features(H1,J),phase=1,Y_J), with equal phase weights and preferably total weight one per pair. A shared-slope logistic plus phase indicator is a simple task-alignment/calibration control; separate heads or interactions are more flexible variants if specified before testing. Item features, scaler, calibration, and any shrinkage coefficient must be learned without confirmation-test outcomes.

This control is not a novel KT algorithm. A phase intercept can nearly align mean predictions with the common outcome prevalence in each training phase. Near-zero mean update after this calibration is partly a modeling constraint; it does not prove that an invalid learning estimate has been corrected. Evaluate held-out proper scores, calibration and downstream decisions, and report both transported and task-aligned models.

## Why causal learning is not identified

A causal estimand might instead be

    tau = E[Y_{J*,T}^{continue regime} - Y_{J*,T}^{stop regime} | B],

where B is baseline information defined before intervention, J* is a fixed or explicitly randomized assessment item bank, T is a defined horizon, and both continuation and stopping are fully specified instructional regimes. Outcomes must refer to comparable assessments under those regimes.

Current logs observe only the factual trajectory and one Y_J. The H0 forecast is not the no-retry potential outcome. Duration, hints/external assistance, stopping, persistence and next-item choice may depend on unrecorded ability and motivation. Conditioning on the future chosen J and S can condition on post-intervention variables. Known or estimated action propensities alone would not remove every such problem; a causal analysis would require a justified intervention model, exchangeability/support/consistency assumptions and suitable measurements.

The present experiment identifies a descriptive predictive quantity within its selected observed population. That is a legitimate scientific target when named accurately. It does not identify learning gained from retries, effectiveness of assigned exercises, or educational policy value.
